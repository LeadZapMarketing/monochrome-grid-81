The short answer

Interior design is the planning of a building's inside spaces so they are comfortable, practical and good to look at. A good interior designer works out how you will move through a room, where things are stored, how light falls at different times of day and which materials will last in Malaysia's heat and humidity, before any furniture is chosen.

In Malaysia, interior design is a regulated profession. The title "Interior Designer" is protected by law, so it is worth checking who you hire. (In Malay, an interior designer is a perekabentuk dalaman; the professional body is the Institut Perekabentuk Dalaman Malaysia, or Malaysian Institute of Interior Design, MIID.)

What does an interior designer do?

For a home, the work usually covers:

  • an interior concept that fits the architecture and the way you live;
  • space planning and the furniture layout;
  • materials and colours, chosen for looks, upkeep and the climate;
  • lighting, both everyday light and the mood of each room;
  • built-in cabinets and storage;
  • feature walls, ceilings and the finishing details;
  • coordination with the building's structure and services, so the design can actually be built.

Who can call themselves an interior designer in Malaysia?

Under Part VA of the Architects Act 1967 (Act 117), the Board of Architects Malaysia (Lembaga Arkitek Malaysia, LAM) keeps a Register of Interior Designers. A person who is not registered may not use the title "Interior Designer", advertise themselves as one, or recover fees in court for interior design services.

To register, a designer must pass the route the Board sets (as a Graduate Interior Designer with practical experience and the Board's examination), or be a corporate member of the Malaysian Institute of Interior Design (MIID) or hold a qualification the Board accepts as equivalent. A registered architect is also entitled by the Board to practise as an Interior Designer.

How to check a designer's registration

LAM lists registered individuals and consultancy practices on its website, so you can check a name before you sign anything: use LAM's registration check.

Tip: ask for the designer's LAM registration number and the name it is registered under, and make sure that is the person who will design your home.

Interior designer, design-and-build company or renovation contractor?

Homeowners in Malaysia usually hire for an interior in one of three ways:

  • An interior designer (design only). You pay for the design and drawings, then appoint a contractor to build them. The designer can help you compare contractors' quotations and check the work on site against the drawings.
  • An interior design-and-build company. One company designs and builds under one contract. It is simpler to manage, but the design and the building prices come from the same company, so ask for a clear breakdown of what each part costs.
  • A renovation contractor working from your own ideas. This can suit simple work, such as replacing a kitchen or re-tiling, but nobody is designing the space as a whole or checking the work for you.

Whichever way you choose, the company that carries out the works must be registered with the Construction Industry Development Board (CIDB). Under section 25 of the CIDB Act 1994 (Act 520), no one may carry out construction work, which the Act defines to include renovation and alteration of a building, or hold themselves out as a contractor, unless they are registered with CIDB. Under section 29, anyone convicted of breaking this rule can be fined between RM10,000 and RM100,000.

How interior design fees are charged

When an architectural practice provides interior design, the Architects (Scale of Minimum Fees) Rules 2010 treat it as a supplementary service, and the minimum fee is 10% of the total cost of works. The fee, and what it includes, should be agreed in writing before the work starts. Our guide to architect fees in Malaysia explains how the minimum fee scale works.

How an interior design project runs

  1. Brief. You explain how you live, what is not working today, your must-haves and your budget range. Bring photos of spaces you like.
  2. Site visit and measurements. The designer measures the space and notes the structure, windows, wiring and plumbing that the design must work with.
  3. Concept. A first layout and a mood for the home: materials, colours and the overall look.
  4. Design development. Detailed layouts, ceilings, lighting, built-ins and the selection of finishes.
  5. Drawings for construction. The drawings and specifications that contractors price and build from.
  6. Approvals where needed. Works that touch the structure, the facade or the building's services may need approval from your local council or building management. Ask your designer to confirm this for your project before work starts.
  7. Construction and site checks. The designer checks the work against the drawings and answers contractors' questions.
  8. Handover. A final inspection, a list of defects to fix, then the keys back to you.

Why plan the interior with the architecture

Many problems in finished homes come from designing the inside after the building is fixed: a beam where the wardrobe should go, a socket behind the sofa, a window that floods the TV wall with glare. When the architect and interior designer plan together from the start, the layout, light, storage and structure fit from day one, and fewer changes are needed on site.

Common questions

Can an architect do interior design?

Yes. LAM's registration requirements state that an architect registered with the Board is entitled to practise or carry on business as an Interior Designer.

Do I need council approval for interior works?

It depends on the works. Works that touch the structure, the facade or the building's services may need approval from your local council or building management. Ask your designer to confirm what your project needs before work starts; our house renovation guide explains when a renovation permit is needed.

Should the contractor be registered too?

Yes. The company that carries out the works must be registered with CIDB, as explained above. Ask for its CIDB registration certificate.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Are you registered with the Board of Architects Malaysia? Under what name?
  • Which stages of the process above are included, and which are not?
  • Is the fee a percentage of the cost of works or a fixed sum, and what does it cover?
  • Who checks the work on site, and how often?
  • Is the contractor registered with CIDB?
  • Can I see completed homes similar in size and style to mine?
